# Legal Foundations for Web3

Reference knowledge on the legal concepts that recur when building a network, a token,
and a company. This is general information, not legal advice; consult qualified counsel
for any real decision.

## Entities and liability

A company is usually organized as a corporation or an LLC to separate the business's
liabilities from its owners' personal assets. The entity, not the founder, signs
contracts, holds IP, and bears obligations. Keeping that separation intact ("not
piercing the corporate veil") requires real separation: distinct finances, proper
records, and acting in the entity's name.

## Contracts

A contract is an agreement with offer, acceptance, and consideration (something of value
exchanged) that the parties intend to be binding. Key terms to read: scope, payment,
term and termination, representations and warranties, indemnification (who covers whom
for what harm), limitation of liability, and governing law. Get it in writing; a signed
document is the record of what was agreed.

## Intellectual property

Copyright protects original expression (including source code) automatically on
creation. Patents protect inventions but require filing. Trademarks protect brand
identifiers. Trade secrets protect confidential information kept secret. Assign IP
explicitly: employees and contractors should sign agreements assigning work product to
the company, or the company may not own what it paid for. Open-source licenses grant use
under conditions — permissive (MIT, Apache) versus copyleft (GPL) — and must be honored.

## Securities considerations for tokens

Whether a token is a security depends on the facts. The Howey test asks if there is an
investment of money in a common enterprise with an expectation of profit derived from
the efforts of others. Marketing that promises returns from a team's work pushes toward
security treatment. Distributing a token for its utility, avoiding profit promises, and
decentralizing over time are factors counsel weigh. Structure and disclosures matter;
this is a domain where professional advice is essential.

## Privacy and data

Handling personal data triggers obligations under regimes such as GDPR and CCPA:
collect only what you need, tell people what you collect and why, secure it, and honor
deletion and access requests. Privacy-by-design — minimizing and encrypting data,
keeping servers blind where possible — reduces both risk and obligation.

## Disclosures and honesty

Public statements to investors, customers, and users must be accurate and not
misleading. Overstating traction, security posture, or capabilities creates legal
exposure on top of the trust cost. Say what is true, disclose what is material, and keep
evidence for the claims you make.
